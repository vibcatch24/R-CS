Mathematical Appendix: Octahedral Symmetry, Equivalence, and Reversibility in R'CS

This appendix formalizes the group-theoretic model behind the R'CS (Rubik's Cube Signature) analytical toolkit (SymmetryAnalyzer.js) and summarizes the structural invariants verified across the reference datasets of Herbert Kociemba and Thomas Rokicki.

1. Fixed-Center State Space and Catch-24 Uniqueness

In R'CS, the six face centers (U, D, F, B, L, R) are fixed in standard WCA orientation; whole-cube rotations (x, y, z) are not part of the state space. Every legal 3×3×3 Rubik's Cube configuration is represented uniquely on the cubie level as an element:

P = (πc, σc, πe, σe) ∈ G,      |G| = 43,252,003,274,489,856,000

where πc ∈ S8 and πe ∈ S12 denote corner and edge permutations, and σc ∈ ℤ38 and σe ∈ ℤ212 denote corner and edge orientations subject to standard parity and orientation-sum constraints.

The 18-character Catch-24 mapping S : G → {a, b, …, x}18 is strictly injective on G: every valid sticker net in fixed WCA orientation corresponds to a single, unique 18-character signature, independent of the move sequence used to reach P.

2. Symmetry Equivalence Criterion and Orbit Compression in Solvers

2.1. Criterion of Symmetry Equivalence

Let Oh (|Oh| = 48) be the full octahedral symmetry group of the cube, consisting of 24 proper rotations (SO3) and 24 improper reflections/rotoinversions (Oh ∖ SO3).

Two fixed-center positions P1, P2 ∈ G are symmetry-equivalent (P1 ~ P2) if and only if one is obtained from the other by conjugation with an element g ∈ Oh:

P2 = g · P1 · g−1,      g ∈ Oh

Operationally, if P1 is generated by a move sequence W, then P2 is generated by the same move sequence W after uniformly relabeling the face letters {U, D, F, B, L, R} (and inverting move directions when g is a reflection) according to g.

2.2. Invariants of an Equivalence Class (Orbit)

All positions in the symmetry orbit 𝒪(P) = { g · P · g−1 | g ∈ Oh } share identical structural invariants:

  1. Identical optimal distance to the solved state in all move metrics (t, q, f).
  2. Identical Schoenflies symmetry group and reversibility indicator (↩ or ⇄).
  3. Isomorphic Cayley graph neighborhoods: For example, the three symmetry-equivalent 26q* (QTM) antipode positions (symmetry group D4h, aligned along U–D, F–B, and R–L) generate three pairwise disjoint local neighborhoods with branching profile 12 – 114 – 1068 down to depth 23q (yielding 3 × 12 = 36, 3 × 114 = 342, and 3 × 1,068 = 3,204 strictly distinct Catch-24 signatures), where the three independent sets of 114 states at 24q act as bridges to 26q*.

2.3. Solver State-Space Reduction

By the Orbit–Stabilizer Theorem, the size of the symmetry orbit of P is:

|𝒪(P)| = 48 / |StabOh(P)|

When algebraic inversion (P ↦ P−1, which also preserves optimal move length) is combined with Oh conjugation, an asymmetric state (|StabOh(P)| = 1) without reversibility represents up to 48 × 2 = 96 equivalent states. In practical solvers, exploiting symmetries yields substantial reductions:

  • Korf's IDA* solver (0 cube symmetries): no symmetry compression.
  • Kociemba's Two-Phase Algorithm (16 axial symmetries, D4h): reduces Phase 1 to the subgroup ⟨U, D, R2, L2, F2, B2⟩ of size 19,508,428,800.
  • Michael Feather's Three-Axis Two-Phase Solver (all 48 symmetries, Oh): drives Phase 1 directly into the half-turn subgroup ⟨U2, D2, R2, L2, F2, B2⟩ of size 3,981,312 — a 4,900× greater reduction of the Phase 2 space.

3. Schoenflies Point Group Classification (Symmetry)

For any state P ∈ G, R'CS computes its exact stabilizer subgroup directly on the cubie representation:

StabOh(P) = { g ∈ Oh | g · P · g−1 = P }

Based on the order, rotation axes, mirror planes, central inversion, and rotoinversion elements (S4, S6) present in StabOh(P), R'CS classifies P into one of the 31 achievable Schoenflies point symmetry groups of the 3×3×3 cube (matching Herbert Kociemba's crystallographic classification).

4. Reversibility and Antisymmetry Supergroups (↩ and {H̃})

Let H = StabOh(P) be the ordinary symmetry group of P, and let P−1 denote the inverse cubie state generated by the inverse move sequence. Because StabOh(P−1) = H, the inverse state always belongs to the same Schoenflies symmetry class as P. R'CS evaluates reversibility across all 48 operations of Oh:

  1. Direct Involution (↩):
    S(P) = S(P−1)  ⇔  P = P−1   (P2 = E)
    The inverse move sequence generates the exact same fixed-center configuration (denoted {I} in Kociemba's tables); applying the generating algorithm twice returns the cube to the solved state.
  2. Antisymmetry Supergroup ({H̃}, e.g., S4 {C4h}):
    A = { g ∈ Oh | g · P · g−1 = P−1 } ≠ ∅,      H̃ = H ∪ A ≤ Oh,      |H̃| = 2|H|
    When P ≠ P−1 and A is non-empty, the union H̃ = H ∪ A forms a closed subgroup of Oh of doubled order (a magnetic/Shubnikov antisymmetry group, containing both proper and improper operations that map P onto P−1). R'CS identifies the exact Schoenflies class of H̃ and displays it in braces next to H (for example, S4 {C4h}).

Whenever P exhibits either ↩ or {H̃}, the inverse state P−1 already lies inside the 48-element orbit 𝒪(P), so the combined orbit size is 48 / |H| rather than 96 / |H|.

  1. Direct Involution (↩):
    S(P) = S(P−1)  ⇔  P = P−1   (P2 = E)
    The inverse move sequence generates the exact same fixed-center configuration; applying the generating algorithm twice returns the cube to the solved state.
  2. Mirror Anti-Involution (⇄):
    S(P) ≠ S(P−1)   and   ∃ M ∈ Oh ∖ SO3 :   S(M · P−1 · M−1) = S(P)
    The inverse move sequence generates a mirror-reflected twin of P (an improper reflection M maps P−1 onto P).

Whenever P exhibits either ↩ or ⇄, the inverse state P−1 already lies inside the 48-element orbit 𝒪(P), so the combined orbit size is 48 / |StabOh(P)| rather than 96 / |StabOh(P)|.
